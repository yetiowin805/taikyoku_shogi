# Further NNUE speed opportunities

Research date: 20 September 2026. The selected next-run baseline is native compilation + cached ability-vector sums + saved accumulator undo. Every future timing comparison should use that combination as its control. None of the ideas below has a measured whole-search speedup over that baseline yet. The live tournament and checkpoints were not changed.

The source review focused on Stockfish, YaneuraOu, and the Rust engine Viridithas. Their techniques are useful precedents, but their architectures, boards and activation distributions differ from ours. In particular, copying Stockfish's sparse path or 16-bit accumulator type is not automatically appropriate.

## Highest-priority experiments

1. **Packed 8-bit output-layer kernels and load-time weight layout.** Our native binary already uses vector instructions. Disassembly of `Accumulator::residual` shows byte-to-32-bit expansion and `vpdpwssd`; it is not scalar code waiting for a compiler flag. A purpose-built kernel can instead keep unsigned activations and signed weights packed as bytes, process several output neurons together, and prearrange weights once at load time. Preserve bias addition, clipping, division/rounding, and the final f32 accumulation order. Existing checkpoints can stay byte-for-byte unchanged. Stockfish explicitly rearranges weight storage and uses byte dot-product helpers; Viridithas also stores aligned, permuted network parameters. This is the most promising next arithmetic experiment, but gains may be limited by move generation and memory costs. [Stockfish affine layer](https://github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/blob/master/src/nnue/layers/affine_transform_sparse_input.h), [Viridithas network layout](https://github.com/cosmobobak/viridithas/blob/master/src/nnue/network.rs).

2. **Skip NNUE work during rule-only royal-evasion probes.** In our `move_resolves_last_royal_check`, the engine makes a candidate move, checks board attacks/royals, and unmakes it. No evaluation is needed, yet `make_move_for_search` updates the neural accumulator and saves undo sums. Suspend derived evaluation bookkeeping inside this narrowly scoped operation, restoring it on every exit. Preserve board state, move order, history, repetition, draw counters and all captures. This is a concrete local opportunity, especially in forced royal tactics; it is our inference from the call path, not a claimed optimization copied from another engine. A small scoped bypass is simpler to test before implementing fully lazy evaluation.

3. **Lazy per-ply accumulator storage, with fused parent-to-child updates.** Record a move's actual changed pieces and materialize the child's accumulator only if evaluation or a needed descendant requires it. A TT cutoff, repetition/draw return, terminal result, or rule-only probe may need no neural work. Keep the parent's accumulator intact, compute a child from it in one tiled pass applying the old mover, captures and final promoted mover, and undo by moving the stack pointer. This builds on saved undo while avoiding its separate copy pass. Stockfish's accumulator stack records dirty state, computes on demand, and uses tiled combined updates; YaneuraOu has tiled source-to-destination updates too. Our multi-leg captures and multiple royals require our own complete delta records. This is a larger correctness-sensitive change, and its marginal gain overlaps the existing cache and undo improvements. [Stockfish accumulator stack and updates](https://github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/blob/master/src/nnue/nnue_accumulator.cpp), [YaneuraOu transformer](https://github.com/yaneurao/YaneuraOu/blob/master/source/eval/nnue/nnue_feature_transformer.h).

4. **Memoize the raw neural residual.** YaneuraOu retains a computed score in the position's accumulator and optionally uses an evaluation hash. Start with per-state/per-perspective residual caching and restoration through undo, then consider a bounded cache across repeated positions if hit rates warrant it. Cache only the pure network output, identified by model/feature contents and board/perspective; retain current material accumulation and terminal/draw checks outside it. This avoids conflating evaluator caching with search TT bounds or repetition-dependent results. Our material uses floating-point incremental accumulation, so caching the entire evaluation by board hash could silently change existing rounding behavior. Benefit depends on actual duplicate evaluations; measure hit opportunities first. [YaneuraOu evaluator](https://github.com/yaneurao/YaneuraOu/blob/master/source/eval/nnue/evaluate_nnue.cpp).

5. **Keep the ability-vector cache warm across searches.** The accepted prototype clears its cache on every accumulator clone. A bounded, exclusively owned per-worker workspace could retain its rows when the model is unchanged, and reuse a contiguous/aligned allocation. Bind it to immutable model contents and feature schema, retain at most one idle workspace per worker, and provide a fresh workspace for nested searches. This is an extension of our own cache; Stockfish's per-thread caches provide precedent for the ownership pattern, not an identical cached object. Measure hits, misses and collisions before increasing its size. [Stockfish cache ownership](https://github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/blob/master/src/nnue/nnue_accumulator.h).

## Small, clean additions

6. **Use exact 32-bit activation arithmetic.** The current conversion is `clamp((i64(a) * 127 + 2048) / 4096, 0, 127)`. The equivalent expression is `(clamp(a, 0, 4096) * 127 + 2048) >> 12`. For values below zero the old output is zero, above 4096 it is 127; inside that interval the intermediate is nonnegative and at most 522240. Therefore it fits i32 and shifting gives the exact quotient. The identity was checked on 1,200,003 boundary, exhaustive-near-zero and random i32 inputs, with no differences. This is a proof-backed simplification, not a measured speedup; it should make activation packing cheaper alongside the new kernel.

7. **Specialize kernels for our five widths and improve layout.** Mature implementations use architecture-specific alignment and fixed network dimensions. Dispatch once per model to width-specific kernels rather than repeatedly resolving generic lengths, tile updates to fit registers, and test a contiguous row cache instead of thousands of separately allocated vectors. Preserve the variable-width fallback. These are possible additions to the packed-kernel/cache work, not independent gains to add together. More code and reserved memory must earn their place. [Stockfish vector tiling](https://github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/blob/master/src/nnue/simd.h).

8. **Stop maintaining unused handcrafted positional terms for NNUE.** `EvalInc::add/sub` still calculates PST and undeveloped-piece terms even though NNUE consumes only its material totals. Preserve the current material arithmetic and order. Include evaluator mode in validity checks or rebuild when switching back to handcrafted evaluation, so matching weight seeds cannot reuse incomplete totals. Likely modest, but mechanically straightforward; this comes from our code rather than an external engine.

## Conditional or lower-priority ideas

9. **Narrow accumulators to 16 bits only with a bound or safe fallback.** Stockfish stores its principal accumulator in i16. Our 32-position sample stayed between -2315 and 6551 across the five models, but that is not a safety proof for hundreds of pieces, promotions, large captures or different checkpoints. Our loader permits much larger weights/biases. A naive type substitution can silently overflow. Investigate model-specific bounds or an exact wide fallback before claiming this is mechanical. Row-cache sums need their own bound too. [Stockfish accumulator types](https://github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/blob/master/src/nnue/nnue_accumulator.h).

10. **Memory-page and prefetch tuning.** Large tables may benefit from large-page allocation and selective prefetching on the VPS, especially with four concurrent players. YaneuraOu loads networks into large-page/shared storage and Viridithas has shared network-weight machinery. We already share immutable `Arc<Network>` objects within a process, so count that existing benefit correctly. This is a secondary experiment requiring cache/TLB measurements and real four-worker validation, not a reason to change system settings now. [YaneuraOu loading](https://github.com/yaneurao/YaneuraOu/blob/master/source/eval/nnue/evaluate_nnue.cpp), [Viridithas loading](https://github.com/cosmobobak/viridithas/blob/master/src/nnue/network.rs).

11. **Sparse output-layer execution and neuron permutation: poor fit for the present checkpoints.** Stockfish skips inactive input blocks; its trainer can reorder neurons to create more all-zero groups. We inspected all five v2 nets on 32 saved positions in four consecutive eight-ply windows. Only 0.18–0.36% of inputs to the first dense layer were zero, and no existing group of four was all zero. A same-sample frequency-sort diagnostic still created at most about 0.2% zero blocks. This is too little evidence of useful sparsity to prioritize a sparse kernel. The positions are correlated game positions, not a search-leaf distribution, so broader telemetry can revisit the decision. [Stockfish sparse layer](https://github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/blob/master/src/nnue/layers/affine_transform_sparse_input.h), [Stockfish neuron permutation tool](https://github.com/official-stockfish/nnue-pytorch/blob/master/ftperm.py).

12. **Architecture/training changes for later.** Smaller output heads or deliberately sparser activations might improve the speed/strength tradeoff, but require new training and tournament comparisons. They are not mechanical optimizations. The current trainer starts the feature bias at 0.5 and clips to [0,1], then centers values in the next layer; that is consistent with the nearly dense activations observed. It does not prove the network is bad. Incrementally updating the dense head is also less immediately attractive: in 28 consecutive transitions per model, only 38–40% of quantized inputs stayed unchanged, and only 3.2–3.5% of four-value groups did. Maintaining both side-to-move projections adds cost. Treat these as longer-term experiments, not first choices.

Finny tables are also not a direct priority: their important use is avoiding full refresh after king-dependent feature changes. Our features are square/ability based and moving a royal does not invalidate every other piece's features. The persistent-cache ownership idea transfers; the king-square table itself does not solve the same problem here.

## Next measurement batch

Start with cheap counters on the accepted combined baseline: time in accumulator updates versus the output head, updates that precede no evaluation, rule-only probes, repeated residual requests, and row-cache hit/miss/collision rates. Collect diagnostic counters separately from timing.

Test packed byte arithmetic, the exact activation conversion, and the royal-probe bypass independently first. Follow with lazy/fused accumulator storage and residual caching where counters justify them. Retain the user's three-second emphasis, use shuffled paired comparisons on quiet, capture-heavy, forced-royal, transposition-heavy and long-history positions, and require fixed-depth score/route/root-order/node parity before timing claims. No expected percentage is assigned until it is measured against the combined baseline; optimizing one stage cannot remove the time spent in other stages.
