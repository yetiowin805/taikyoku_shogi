What looks worth improving

  1. Quiet defenses and capturing the attacker — Start with 817/43, 673/174, 756/396 and 869/25. These have concrete threats and same-depth support for a defense. Check defensive move reductions and the depth allowed to non-PV quiescence before assuming capture generation is missing.
  2. Compound capture of all royals — 864/484 has an exact Lion Hawk route taking both remaining White royals. Test emergency detection for all-royal captures even when two royals remain; a “last royal only” check flag does not describe this danger.
  3. Full exchange and path accounting — Keep the profitable/equal sacrifices as controls. Include all enemy and friendly path victims, recaptures, and the second threat elsewhere on the board. Do not teach the search to reject every defended landing.
  4. Promotion and quiet threat preparation — Free King becoming Great General repeatedly creates new sweep access. Shitennou’s quiet approach and ordinary quiet escapes require main-search coverage; simply adding capture plies is not guaranteed to help.
  5. Forcing intermediate moves — 756/332 is a checking intermediate capture that postpones threats against two majors. Preserve check/evasion treatment; a superficially safer quiet move can concede the forcing tempo.
Scope, evidence standards and limitations

All 32 shortlisted cases are reviewed individually. A verified motif means the move/capture geometry or forcing royal condition was checked through the engine rules. It does not prove that the earlier mistake was avoidable, or that quiescence caused it.

The report distinguishes the previously isolated Capricorn failure, defenses supported by controlled depth-4 comparisons, other verified patterns with unresolved defenses, useful compensation/forcing-move controls, and an unresolved score-drop case.

New checks: 108 historical-engine searches (depth 4 unrestricted, depth 4 restricted to the played move, and depth 3 with two quiescence plies on all leaves, plus targeted follow-ups). Search uses the original agent’s NNUE model; it is not an independent tactical oracle. Incomplete iterations are explicitly marked. Scores are own-perspective internal units, not calibrated centipawns.

Counterfactual threat maps temporarily give the opponent the turn; they identify available capture geometry, not a legal pass or a forced line. Actual proposed moves and PVs were then replayed with the engine’s last-royal evasion handling. Capture lists retain up to twelve routes removing at least 300 seed material units or a royal; they are not exhaustive lists of all small captures.

GUI plies are 1-based, and a position is BEFORE that move. Route coordinates are the engine’s 0-based file,rank pairs. Seed material is used only to describe exchanges; it is different from NNUE scores and does not establish overall positional value.

The three reserved games were not searched. Cases within one game or shared opening/threat family remain correlated; do not count them as independent tournament-strength measurements.

90/108 searches completed their requested depth. Models and historical search binary match the previous corpus; raw logs, plans, hashes and rule-check results are stored beside this report.
