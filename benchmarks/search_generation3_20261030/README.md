# Third search generation: recent HLF/HLFS games and a bounded trial portfolio

**Status: implemented and locally screened; 24 derived identities prepared, not launched as of this report draft.** The two foundations remain HLF and HLFS with the same W512-v3 network parameters. There is no tournament-strength verdict for this portfolio. The first screen's PR implementation was subsequently tightened. A final guarded PR rerun completed on the same nine positions; no partial adoptions occurred. The original PR row below is retained only as an initial-screen result, not a test of the refined partial-adoption condition. Deployment/admission status will be recorded separately by the operator.

The useful next step is a broad but interpretable tournament wave: preserve both S and non-S foundations, include a shared context-repair control, then test cheaper search, better selection and a few richer tactical policies. Recent games provide concrete reasons to investigate quiet major-piece attacks, constricted escape routes, quiet mating approaches, and incomplete iterations. They do not support simply searching every capture or treating every large raw/search disagreement as a mistake.

## What the recorded games suggest

Large raw/search differences deserve classification. In the 12,022-turn corrected sample, the absolute gap has median **232**, p90 **1,029**, p95 **1,358**, and p99 **2,386**. There are 197 gaps of at least 2,000; 33 accompany literal immediate recaptures, 42 capture the previous mover, 108 choose some enemy capture, and four have search score zero. These categories overlap. An immediate recapture here specifically means the previous move removed our piece and the chosen move removes the enemy on the previous destination; it does not classify every cause of a tactical gap. Two of the most extreme gaps are obvious recaptures, but most large gaps are not explained by that label alone. [Gap classification](/home/frank/taikyoku_shogi/data/analysis/search-generation3-20261030/mining/large-gap-classification.json).

Actual early depth is often **three or four**, rather than five. Among 1,400 sampled target turns before root ply 100, 633 finish depth three, 626 depth four, and only 58 finish depth five or greater. Among completed depth-three-to-four transitions in this phase, iteration duration grows by a median **8.59×**, p90 **26.07×** (684 observations). Completed next iterations are a selected subset: incomplete attempts are censored, so excluding them does not establish the unconditional growth distribution. The old admission predictor uses three times the preceding iteration duration. This discrepancy motivates a clock-policy trial, not a claim that a particular multiplier is optimal. [Recorded depth/growth](/home/frank/taikyoku_shogi/data/analysis/search-generation3-20261030/mining/summary.json), [clock admission](/home/frank/taikyoku_shogi/.worktrees/search-generation3/src/training/clock.rs:65).

**Incomplete iterations consume 35.9% of recorded iteration wall time** in these corrected nonmate target turns. They occur on 3,381/12,022 turns (28.1%). Before root 100 the corresponding figures are 17.0% of time and 4.9% of turns; roots 100–399 are 38.7%/26.8%; roots 400 onward are 44.1%/33.7%. This denominator is summed iteration telemetry, not total engine setup, game wall time, or an estimate of directly recoverable speed. The original policy returns the previous completed iteration except for an initial partial-depth fallback. Completed sibling results from an interrupted later iteration therefore usually do not affect that turn's returned move. They can still populate search caches and reveal information, so “incomplete work” is more accurate than assuming every node is wasted. [Timing analysis](/home/frank/taikyoku_shogi/data/analysis/search-generation3-20261030/mining/incomplete-iteration-cost.json), [original fallback and new opt-in PR path](/home/frank/taikyoku_shogi/.worktrees/search-generation3/src/search.rs:2809).

The opening pattern described by the user is visible quantitatively. Among 280 HLF/HLFS turns in the first 20 plies of corrected games, **82 remove friendly blockers**: FierceDragon 34, GreatGeneral 27, ViceGeneral 17, BishopGeneral two and FlyingGeneral two. Most remove six or seven friendly pieces. The early same-player absolute search swing has median 602, p90 2,338 and p99 3,748. These observations support preserving useful lane-clearing sacrifices, and investigating the survival and follow-up of the deployed attacker. They do not justify a blanket penalty on friendly removal. [Opening measurements](/home/frank/taikyoku_shogi/data/analysis/search-generation3-20261030/mining/opening-metrics.json).

## Engine precedents and what transfers

Stockfish's tactical picker combines capture history with victim value, stages TT/captures/quiets, partially sorts quiets, and rewards checks and escapes from lesser attackers. The useful transfer is selective ordering and deferred work, not a chess-sized dense table on a 36×36 board. [Stockfish 18 move picker](https://github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/blob/sf_18/src/movepick.cpp).

Stockfish also uses correction history, futility margins, null verification and other selective depth decisions. YaneuraOu's maintained shogi search contains analogous futility/ProbCut machinery, tactical verification and move-count restrictions in qsearch. A small tactical allowance is therefore not itself evidence of a poor design: the important questions are eligibility, ordering and exceptions. Constants fitted to ordinary chess or shogi are not validated Ultimate Shogi parameters. [Stockfish 18 search](https://github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/blob/sf_18/src/search.cpp), [YaneuraOu search](https://github.com/yaneurao/YaneuraOu/blob/master/source/engine/yaneuraou-engine/yaneuraou-search.cpp).

Ethereal supplies another useful reference for scout/research, reductions and updating an incumbent from a partial deeper search, including reverting it after fail-low. Our PR trial deliberately uses stricter same-attempt completed-line checks; this report does not claim it reproduces Ethereal. [Ethereal search and root-line handling](https://github.com/AndyGrant/Ethereal/blob/master/src/search.c).

The largest general constraints are branching factor, generation cost and the depth available before the clock stops. Complete routes, friendly and multiple victims, promotion origin and surviving royal count constrain correctness. Jumpers and two-movers make cheap geometry a useful prescreen but an insufficient proof of attack. The episodes also contain ordinary sliding mates and quiet development; a special-mover-only heuristic would miss much of the useful search work.

## Prepared portfolio: twelve trials on both foundations

Identity format is `NNUE_W512_v3_{HLF|HLFS}_G3{label}`: **24 new identities**, preserving the two existing parents as additional controls. Each uses its parent's unchanged network weights. The `trial` selector is separate from the existing HLF/HLFS policy, so S remains an explicit experimental factor.

**All non-None trials include the incoming-context restoration tested by CF.** A descendant search could overwrite capture loudness, previous landing, wipe/large-mover flags and q entry depth before a retry. New trials save/restore these at the recursive boundary. To attribute a feature, compare it with its own foundation's **CF**, not only with the historical parent. Old agents with missing/None trial retain their old policy. This is a common prerequisite with its own control, not twelve independent single changes. [Context restoration](/home/frank/taikyoku_shogi/.worktrees/search-generation3/src/search.rs:3565), [trial definitions](/home/frank/taikyoku_shogi/.worktrees/search-generation3/src/eval.rs:1156).

- **CF / ContextRetry:** repair retry context only. Low memory cost; recursive snapshot/restore overhead. This isolates the common prerequisite and can itself change tactical admission. It is necessary to interpret the other arms.
- **RF / QRefill:** inspect up to three times the ordinary q candidate allowance, charging the search quota after live delta, hanging and make filters accept a move. Royal captures, loud promotions and required major destination recaptures retain exemptions. Small counters/vector work; extra rejected-candidate attack checks can cost time. Promising where high-ranked candidates are rejected, but inert when the original selected moves already survive. [Quota logic](/home/frank/taikyoku_shogi/.worktrees/search-generation3/src/search.rs:4636).
- **QH / QHistory:** add existing capture history to q ordering while retaining eligibility and quota. Reuses the bounded table; no new dense history memory. With tiny q quotas, better ranking changes which exchange is seen. History can be noisy or insufficiently trained, and reordering can change selective values. [Scoring](/home/frank/taikyoku_shogi/.worktrees/search-generation3/src/search.rs:4594).
- **PVS / InternalPvs:** scout later interior moves and widen promising results. Existing child PV context is retained, including the first Stage B quirk, so the trial isolates windowing as far as that selective search permits. Little persistent memory, potential node savings, but repeated fail-highs can cost more. [Scout and retry](/home/frank/taikyoku_shogi/.worktrees/search-generation3/src/search.rs:4030).
- **PV / PromotePv:** give improving later moves richer PV treatment on retry. Main TT scores cannot immediately answer a PV retry from a shallower scout horizon; move hints remain usable. This can find tactics omitted by non-PV leaves, but also spends more time and disables some TT score reuse. It is intentionally a tactical-policy change, not just a window optimization. [PV TT guard](/home/frank/taikyoku_shogi/.worktrees/search-generation3/src/search.rs:3620).
- **SC / SearchedLmr:** base reduction thresholds on moves actually searched rather than positions in a list that may contain rejected moves; carry the searched count across stages. Negligible counters, potentially fewer harmful early reductions, but greater total search where many earlier candidates were rejected. [Move count](/home/frank/taikyoku_shogi/.worktrees/search-generation3/src/search.rs:3897).
- **FP / ShallowFutility:** at non-PV depth one after a quiet non-wipe incoming move, allow a static-beta cutoff with an 800-unit margin. Exclude mate windows, last-royal check, a current royal capture and a credible hanging-major take. It can shave unpromising branches but remains a heuristic: quiet preparations like the discovered cases are precisely its risk. No new persistent memory. [Guards](/home/frank/taikyoku_shogi/.worktrees/search-generation3/src/search.rs:3640).
- **PR / PartialRoot:** after interruption, adopt a better fully verified root line only if the old full-route incumbent and alternative were completed in the same depth/aspiration attempt. Scores must exceed that candidate's starting alpha and remain below beta. Keep completed depth distinct from optional selected depth. This uses existing completed work with a small root-line vector; uneven-depth bounds and telemetry are its main correctness risks. The guard was refined after the initial screen; the final rerun produced no partial adoptions in the selected cases. [Selection and bounds](/home/frank/taikyoku_shogi/.worktrees/search-generation3/src/search.rs:2774), [incumbent comparison](/home/frank/taikyoku_shogi/.worktrees/search-generation3/src/search.rs:3432).
- **ATK / MajorThreatLmr:** reduce less when a quiet move creates a verified new attack on a previously unattacked enemy major. Cheap geometry selects possible targets; the post-move oracle verifies the attack. The trapped HookMover and BishopGeneral cases motivate it. It allocates more depth rather than pruning, and repeated attack queries can be expensive. It does not specifically prove a target is trapped or a threat wins. [Threat oracle](/home/frank/taikyoku_shogi/.worktrees/search-generation3/src/search/experiments.rs:414).
- **RFQH / QRefillHistory:** combine refill and ordering. If history moves a surviving tactic into the original quota, refill may add little; if history's favorite is rejected, refill can complement it. Extra checks and history-sensitive selection are the risks. Its interpretation comes from RF and QH, not from treating it as an unrelated candidate.
- **PVSPV / PvsPromote:** combine interior scouts with richer retry treatment. Potentially pair cheap rejection with better scrutiny of promising lines; potentially pay both repeated scout/research and richer q/TT costs. PVS and PV provide the required ablations.
- **GA / GrowthAware:** use observed completed-iteration growth, clamped to 3–16, to decide whether to start another iteration under the Fischer budget. Retain sustainable/hard caps and the one-extension policy. Small duration state; it can reduce expensive interrupted attempts but may stop before a crucial depth that would have finished. Its actual clock behavior is **not exercised by fixed-time screen requests**, which have no Fischer soft budget. [Growth policy](/home/frank/taikyoku_shogi/.worktrees/search-generation3/src/search.rs:2509).

No trial adds a new network or a board-squared continuation table. “Small memory cost” is source-derived mechanism accounting, not an RSS measurement. The tournament admission path and finalized validation must still be checked before claiming all prepared identities are running.

## Additional promising routes, kept separate from this portfolio

**A post-SimpleTake hanging test deserves its own future paired arm.** During focused fixture work, the source author reproduced a limitation of the existing pre-move attack oracle: querying a square occupied by White's Bishop for White attacks returns false, while after Black captures that Bishop the same square returns true for its White Rook defender. GreatGeneral and GoldGeneral defenders show the same own-occupied/enemy-occupied distinction. The current SimpleTake hang filter queries before the capture, so it can miss defenders on an enemy's currently occupied square. The regression documents the contract; **no production post-SimpleTake fix or new arm is included here**. A post-move check could prune more losing captures and improve speed, but must account for removed defenders, pins, promotion and legal counterplay; it should be tested separately rather than silently changing all controls. [Regression](/home/frank/taikyoku_shogi/.worktrees/search-generation3/src/search/experiments.rs:685), [q pre-make filter](/home/frank/taikyoku_shogi/.worktrees/search-generation3/src/search.rs:4766), [main capture check](/home/frank/taikyoku_shogi/.worktrees/search-generation3/src/search.rs:1105).

**Quiet checks and constrained royal flights** are credible next additions. The balanced mating cases involve ordinary sliders and a king with limited safe flights, not an already huge material advantage. A bounded checking stage, ordering bonus, or one forcing continuation could help; admitting every quiet check on this board could explode the tree. Existing `VerifiedFlights` should not be assumed to supply this already: NNUE evaluation returns before the handcrafted last-royal flight term, making that legacy flag inert for the static score despite a large trial coefficient. `MateProbe` and `ScarceDefenses` affect search through different paths and are not thereby inert; a few diagnostic requests are not sufficient to recommend them as new agents. [NNUE early return](/home/frank/taikyoku_shogi/.worktrees/search-generation3/src/eval.rs:1836), [legacy-mode probe provenance](/home/frank/taikyoku_shogi/data/analysis/search-generation3-20261030/royal-probes/legacy-royal-mode-manifest.json).

**Correction history** remains plausible, especially where raw values repeatedly misprice quiet threats. It is deferred from the 24 prepared identities: the first wave already tests how much better search selection/context can recover, and a correction scheme needs a clear update target that does not teach shallow selective errors back into pruning. Stockfish's implementation is precedent for a separate bounded learned correction, not evidence that a material-based correction context or its constants transfer here. [Stockfish correction use](https://github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/blob/sf_18/src/search.cpp).

**Across-turn aged history, staged generation, compact TT replacement and selective exchange estimates** remain useful engineering follow-ups. They could avoid repeated ordering/generation work, but the current evidence does not measure their memory, time or strength effects. Persistent score bounds require draw-history and selective-horizon care; persistent move hints are easier to isolate. A safe-landing/trapped-major evaluation term also deserves separate consideration, because legal route counts can be enormous while useful escapes are scarce.

**Radical search is established elsewhere, but is a different project.** Leela Chess Zero uses MCTS/PUCT guided by network policy and value outputs. Our present NNUE supplies a value, without that learned full-route policy head. On this branching factor, replacing alpha–beta with untrained/uniform move priors would not reproduce Leela's advantages; a policy network and suitable training/admission route would be substantial work. Defer that change while the cheaper alpha–beta opportunities are testable. [Lc0 overview](https://lczero.org/dev/overview/), [policy/value and PUCT description](https://lczero.org/dev/lc0/search/alphazero/).
